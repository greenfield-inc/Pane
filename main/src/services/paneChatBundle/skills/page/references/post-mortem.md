# Implementation post-mortem

Write a short HTML page for the person after implementation ends. Lead with the outcome and whether the original feature actually works. Keep the approved plan as the historical baseline and describe the result against it.

- **Outcome:** completed, blocked, or failed; delivered behavior, unfinished requirements, tested revision, and links to the plan, PR, verification evidence, and conversation viewer when there is one.
- **How implementation went:** brief account by stage or milestone, including what was reused or created and what worked well.
- **Where it got stuck:** concrete blockers, unsuccessful attempts, root cause when known, how it recovered, or what remains unresolved. Link relevant trace moments; distinguish observed facts from hypotheses.
- **Surprises and deviations:** original approach → actual approach, why it changed, impact on scope, schema, or architecture, and user approval where required. Say explicitly when there were no material deviations.
- **Verification and review:** results against “How we will know it works,” how the change was reviewed, findings and fixes, and remaining gaps. Call behavior complete only when it was tested.
- **Lessons:** a few actionable improvements for future plans or runs, based on this evidence. Include time, token, or cost figures only when measured and scoped; mark estimates and missing telemetry.

Link back to the hub and the approved plan. Keep it factual, concise, and useful to someone who did not watch the run. Summarize tool output instead of pasting raw logs, and give reasons for delays only when the evidence shows them.
